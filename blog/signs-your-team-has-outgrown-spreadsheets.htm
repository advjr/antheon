Five signs your team has outgrown spreadsheets

Spreadsheets are a great place to start and a costly place to stay. Here is how to tell when the workbook has become the bottleneck.

Antheon Team
7 Oct 20265 min read

On this page

On this page

Key takeaways

  • Version confusion and copy-paste work are the earliest warning signs.
  • If one person is the only one who understands the sheet, that is a business risk.
  • Moving off spreadsheets is about structure first, software second.

Nearly every business we work with started on spreadsheets, and that was the right call. They are flexible, free, and everyone already knows how to use them. The trouble is that spreadsheets grow quietly. One tab becomes twelve, one owner becomes five, and somewhere along the way the tool that helped you move fast starts slowing you down.

Here are the five signs we see most often.

1. Nobody trusts which version is current

"Final", "final v2", "use this one". If your team regularly asks which file is the real one, decisions are being made on data that may already be out of date. Every copy is a chance for two people to work from different numbers.

2. Someone copies data between sheets every week

If a person spends part of every week moving numbers from one sheet to another, that is a process waiting to be automated. It is slow, it is boring, and it is exactly where typos creep in. Copy-paste is the clearest sign that two things should be connected.

If a person is the integration, the integration will eventually break.

3. Only one person really understands it

Most growing teams have a "spreadsheet person" who built the formulas and knows which cells not to touch. That works until they are on leave, busy, or gone. When a single workbook depends on a single person, you have a key-person risk sitting in a shared drive.

4. Reporting takes days, not minutes

Ask a simple question, such as how many jobs are behind schedule, and see how long the answer takes. If it means pulling exports, cleaning them, and building a pivot table, your reporting is a project, not a view. Leaders end up deciding on gut feel because the data arrives too late to help.

5. Mistakes surface after the client does

The most expensive sign is when errors are found by customers: a wrong invoice, a missed booking, a double order. By then the cost is not just the fix. It is the trust.

What to do instead

The answer is not to ban spreadsheets. It is to move the parts that run your operation into a structure built for it, and keep spreadsheets for analysis and one-off work.

  • Find the source of truth. Decide which record is the official one for clients, jobs, and money.
  • Connect, don't copy. Replace manual transfers with links between systems.
  • Make reports live. Build dashboards that read from the source, so the answer is always current.
Start with the most painful sheetYou do not need to replace everything at once. Pick the workbook that causes the most rework or the most risk, and move that first.
